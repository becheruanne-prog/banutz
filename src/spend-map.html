    What you do: the 50/30/20 check

      50/30/20 is the Balanced Money Formula of Elizabeth Warren and Amelia Warren Tyagi (All Your Worth, 2005): up to 50% of income on needs, up to 30% on wants, at least 20% saved. It is published advice, not a scientific test. Needs here are rent, utilities, groceries, fuel, transport, health, pet, school and phone; wants are eating out, going out, impulse buys and other subscriptions.

      Where every rule comes from

      Nothing in your profile is made up. Each part is either validated research, published advice, industry data, or a rule of thumb this app sets, and it says which.

      What the app doesBasisSource
      Money quiz (spender type)Validated researchRick, Cryder & Loewenstein, Journal of Consumer Research 34, 2008, 13,327 people (paper). Used again in Journal of Marketing Research 2011 (Rick, Small & Finkel) and with children in J. Behavioral Decision Making 2018 (Smith et al.). Questions and scoring as published.
      50/30/20 checkPublished adviceWarren & Warren Tyagi, All Your Worth, 2005; the authors on their formula in MONEY, August 2005 (interview).
      Payday spendingResearch + app's ruleSpending rises when expected income arrives: Gelman et al., Science 2014, using data from a personal finance app (summary); Stephens, American Economic Review 2003 (paper). Much of the rise is bills paid on payday, so bills are left out. The 3-day window and the 1.3× level are this app's rule of thumb.
      End-of-month squeezeResearch + app's ruleSpending falls as the next payment nears: Hastings & Washington, AEJ: Economic Policy 2010 (paper), studied on food spending. The last-7-days window and the level are this app's rule of thumb.
      Weekend spendingIndustry data + app's ruleCard data from over 50 million customers shows more spending on Fridays and Saturdays: JPMorgan Chase Institute, 2016 (insight). The 1.2× level is this app's rule of thumb.
      Small treats and coffeeAwareness onlyFrom David Bach's “latte factor”; critics note small cuts alone rarely make anyone wealthy (Helaine Olen, Pound Foolish). Shown to build awareness. The 30 lei and 5-a-week levels are this app's.
      Pay yourself first, 10%Published advice“A part of all you earn is yours to keep. It should be not less than a tenth”: George S. Clason, The Richest Man in Babylon, 1926 (text).
      Shopping, subscriptions, delivery, steady weeksApp's rule of thumbCounted straight from your payments; the levels that flag them are this app's choice.
      Future You growthStandard mathsRegular saving with monthly compounding. The growth rate is an example you set, not a forecast; inflation and taxes are not included.
      CategoriesApp's ruleMatched by shop name; your own choices always win.

      Coming soon

      We don’t save to survive. We save so that the money we keep can start working for us. When your goal is on track and you have a safety cushion, Banutz will walk you through your first steps as an investor.

      1. Build a safety cushion first. A few months of your costs kept aside, so you never have to sell in a hurry.
      2. Learn the basics. What a share, a bond and an index fund are, and why time and fees matter more than luck.
      3. Practise with pretend money. A sandbox where you invest play money and watch it move, with nothing real at risk.
      4. Take your first real step. How to choose a regulated broker and how much of your budget can go in each month.
